Start Here

Photo Digitizing Guide — a plain-English manual for rescuing your family's printed photos, slides, and film negatives into a safe digital archive.

If you have a shoebox of old prints, a carousel of slides, or strips of negatives in an envelope marked "Christmas 1987," this guide is for you. No cloud accounts, no subscriptions, no special expertise required — just a phone, a flatbed scanner (if you have one), and a weekend or two.

What this guide covers

  1. Phone vs. Flatbed: Choosing Your Capture Tool — which tool for which job, and when it doesn't matter.
  2. Lighting Setups — cheap, repeatable lighting that makes phone scans look professional.
  3. Resolution Honesty: 600 dpi vs. 300 dpi — what those numbers actually buy you, and when "good enough" really is.
  4. File Naming: Years and Events — names your grandchildren will understand.
  5. Folder Trees — a simple structure that scales to thousands of photos.
  6. Backup to Two Places — the 3-2-1 rule, adapted for real households.
  7. Sharing with Relatives — getting copies to siblings and cousins without the cloud.
  8. Printing a Keepsake — turning the archive back into something you can hold.
  9. Scanner Worksheets — printable checklists and batch logs.
  10. Glossary — every term used in this guide, defined once.

How to use this guide

  • Have one weekend and a phone? Read chapters 1–3, then 4–6. You'll have a working archive.
  • Have a flatbed scanner? Chapters 1 and 3 tell you when it earns its desk space.
  • Have a relative waiting for photos? Skim 4–5, then jump to 7 (sharing) and 8 (keepsakes).

The one rule that matters most

Every photo should exist in at least two physical places before you call it "saved." A scan on your laptop is not an archive — it's a single copy of something irreplaceable. Chapter 6 makes this painless.
